## Abstract

We propose a minimum standard for **auditable causal inference** — that a causal-effect estimate used for a consequential decision should *terminate exactly within bounded resources, be independently recomputable, carry an explicit justification, and declare its regime* — and supply the empirical evidence that current practice violates the first two criteria. Frontier large language models (LLMs) know textbook causal inference: given a small confounded system in textbook form, GPT-5.1, DeepSeek-v4, Gemini-2.5-Pro and Claude all compute the back-door adjustment correctly. We show that correct causal-effect estimation is nonetheless not primarily a *knowledge* problem but a *computational-terminability* problem. As the number of confounder configurations that an intervention query must marginalize, 2^k, grows, every LLM we tested fails — not by lacking the method, but by being unable to carry out the exact marginalization within its reasoning budget. An explicit intervention engine that performs the marginalization deterministically is exact (accuracy 1.000) across the entire range, in milliseconds, at essentially zero token cost, and attaches to every answer a credential that an independent third-party library (pgmpy) recomputes to within 10⁻⁶. We establish this with three vital numbers under a known causal graph and given conditional-probability tables (CPTs): a confounding-core regression test, a complexity-scaling axis, and a combinatorial-explosion axis. We then relax the toy premises one step at a time — CPTs estimated from finite data, an imperfect adjustment set, and adversarial true-value distributions — and find the engine's advantage survives, while honestly delimiting where it does *not*: at low combinatorial complexity in clean natural language the LLM needs no engine, and a credential certifies computation, **not** structural correctness. The combinatorial cliff holds across model families at any deployable reasoning budget; its *location* slides with capability and budget, and removing it demands extreme token cost — or is impossible for reasoning models that terminate early rather than spend the budget (§7). We argue the contribution is making "which causal output can I trust" a machine-verifiable fact rather than a gamble, within a clearly bounded regime.

> **Scope of every "1.000" and efficiency claim in this paper**: results hold under a *known* causal graph with *given* CPTs (structured input). For natural-language and unknown-structure settings, see §7 (Boundaries).

## 1. Introduction

Causal questions — *what happens to Y if we intervene on X?* — are distinct from predictive ones, and the distinction is the back-door adjustment: under confounding, P(Y|do(X)) ≠ P(Y|X). A decade of work (Pearl, Spirtes, Shpitser) makes the *math* standard. The open question for the LLM era is operational: when a model is asked an interventional query, does it reliably *carry out* the computation?

We separate two failure modes. A **knowledge** failure is not knowing that confounding must be adjusted. A **terminability** failure is knowing the method but being unable to execute it within a fixed reasoning budget. We find frontier models have essentially no knowledge failure on textbook-form problems, and a severe, systematic terminability failure as combinatorial load grows. This reframes the value of an explicit causal layer: not to teach the model causal inference, but to move the combinatorial computation off the autoregressive reasoning chain and onto a deterministic engine that is exact and that emits a verifiable credential.

**What is standard, and what is new.** We state this up front to forestall the obvious objection. The inference the engine performs — exact marginalization / variable elimination over discrete CPTs, and back-door adjustment — is *textbook* and not our contribution; it is available in pgmpy, DoWhy, and any Bayesian-network library, which we use as independent verifiers rather than reinvent. Our contributions are:

1. **A minimum standard for auditable causal inference** (§3) — *our primary, longest-lived contribution*. We distil four criteria a consequential causal-effect estimate should meet — terminability, independent recomputability, explicit justification, declared regime/boundary — each paired with its failure case (criteria 1–2 are empirically failed by LLMs; criterion 4 is our own honest limit). The standard is **separable from any single experiment and from our software** (any causal tool — pgmpy, DoWhy, bnlearn — can conform), and maps directly onto model-risk/regulatory validation (SR 11-7, BCBS 239, IFRS 9/CECL, ECB TRIM; §3.7). A specific model's accuracy dates; a methodological criterion, once adopted, compounds.
2. **The combinatorial-cliff benchmark — the empirical necessity case** (§4, §6). We show, for the first time systematically, that frontier LLMs' failure on interventional queries is *computational terminability*, not knowledge, and we characterize the collapse as a **sharp phase transition** on the 2^k axis (textbook k=1–2 ≈100% → k=4–5 ≈0%) at any deployable reasoning budget. The collapse is consistent across four bases and three families, with **three distinct crash signatures** (§6.3); it is not fixed by prompting (§6.1), is **not a token-length artifact** (a decisive control inverts prompt length while the cliff persists, §6.2), and its origin is model-dependent — premature termination in reasoning models, budget exhaustion in flash models, both sidestepped by the engine (§7). This is the evidence that criteria 1–2 are routinely violated today; the finding *is* the benchmark, variable elimination merely the yardstick.
3. **A verifiable-correctness gradient and its honest limits** (§5–7). The engine's edge survives finite-sample CPTs and imperfect structure; we expose and correct a methodological artifact (anchoring, §6.4); and we delimit precisely what a credential does *not* certify — the structure and the CPT's calibration regime (§5.2, §6.5, §7).

We position this as **auditable causal inference**: the inference is standard, the auditability is the point.

## 1a. Related work

We connect to, and explicitly do not reinvent, several lines:
- **Causal inference & identification** (Pearl 1995/2009; Spirtes et al.; Shpitser & Pearl) — the do-calculus, back-door criterion, and the ID algorithm are the tools we apply. Standard libraries (pgmpy; DoWhy, Sharma & Kiciman 2020) implement the inference; we use pgmpy as an *independent recompute path*, not a competitor.
- **LLM causal benchmarks & the LLM-vs-engine dichotomy** — that LLMs fail at do-queries is established (CLadder, NeurIPS 2023; CausalBench; CaLM), as is the remedy of an explicit engine: *Why LLMs Fail at Causal Discovery and How Interventional Agents Escape* (arXiv:2605.27567) proves a kernel-obstruction theorem and shows a Bayesian-loop agent using a frozen LLM as an *interventional oracle* (A-CBO) escapes the discovery failure on up to 24 variables (close in spirit to our bet③/discovery line); *Can Post-Training Transform LLMs into Causal Reasoners?* (arXiv:2602.06337, CauGym/CaLM) reports that post-trained small models can match or beat much larger raw LLMs on its causal-task suite. We do **not** claim the phenomenon or the dichotomy is new. Our contribution is the *characterization*: prior work reports **continuous degradation** with difficulty; we report a **sharp phase transition** on the single 2^k axis (textbook k=1–2 ≈100% → k=4–5 ≈0%), framed as **exact-marginalization terminability**. The CauGym result is not in tension with ours: its tasks establish that causal *competence* is learnable, whereas our cliff isolates the distinct regime where the method is known but its exact high-k *execution* terminates in failure. **Crucially**, *Frontier LLMs Still Struggle with Simple Reasoning Tasks* (arXiv:2507.07313) — which, like us, scales a parameter that inflates required computation while holding intrinsic difficulty fixed — attributes such collapses partly to long-context degradation and intermediate-step arithmetic errors. We directly rule this out as the driver of *our* cliff: padding low-load (k=2) problems with causally-inert distractor variables to **equal or greater** prompt length than the collapsing high-load case leaves gpt-5.1 accuracy at **1.00** (186 words) while the shorter high-load prompt collapses to **0.00** (177 words); length is controlled, even inverted, and the cliff tracks 2^k (§6.2). The clean length-control holds across **three families** — gpt-5.1, gemini-2.5-pro, and claude-opus-4-8 (whose longest low-load prompt of 506 words scores 1.00 while its shorter 483-word high-load prompt collapses) — with load-sensitivity also replicating on a fourth (deepseek). To our knowledge this sharp-cliff-on-2^k characterization, *with the length confound controlled*, has not been reported as a core result.
- **Produce-with-a-proof lineage**: proof-carrying code (Necula 1997); neuro-symbolic verification (symbolic checks over neural outputs); selective / abstaining prediction (Chow 1970; Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017) — our protocol-failure and near-zero rules are abstention keyed to *verifiable causal identifiability* rather than to uncertainty; algorithmic recourse (Karimi et al.) gives verifiable counterfactual *explanations* whereas we certify the inference itself. Our credential is the first, to our knowledge, to attach a third-party-recomputable justification to a causal *effect estimate*. We claim novelty for the causal-inference application and the recompute credential, not for the produce-with-a-proof idea itself.

## 2. Setup and methods

**Systems.** Binary structural causal models (SCMs) with a treatment X, outcome Y, k independent confounders U₁…U_k each pointing to both X and Y (pure back-door), plus optional causally-inert *distractor* nodes used only to vary prompt length independently of marginalization load (the token-length control, §6.2). The adjustment set is {U₁…U_k}; the interventional target is P(Y=1|do(X=1)) = Σ_u P(Y=1|X=1,u)∏_i P(u_i).

**Subjects.** (A) raw LLM, (B) LLM with a causal-inference scaffold prompt, (C) the explicit engine (exact enumeration / graph surgery). LLMs: gpt-5.1, deepseek-v4-flash, gemini-2.5-pro, claude. Each is given the full SCM in text and asked for a 4-decimal probability.

**Scoring (AM-007).** A protocol failure — no parseable answer within a fixed budget — counts as an error for accuracy (terminability is a capability dimension), is *excluded* (not substituted) from continuous-error means, and its rate is reported separately (AM-007+).

**Verification (the immune system).**
- *R3*: every frozen truth is hand-derived and machine-recomputed; the machine value is authoritative.
- *Implementation gate (IPRG, AM-016)*: the engine's value is recomputed by an independent code path (pgmpy variable elimination + graph surgery); agreement to 10⁻⁶ certifies the absence of implementation bugs.
- *Semantic gate (AM-016+)*: a statement⟺structure⟺output consistency check certifies the absence of specification errors (e.g. a problem text inconsistent with the SCM used as truth). Two gates, each with a defined blind spot, neither sufficient alone.
- *Beautiful Failures*: an append-only log of falsified hypotheses and retractions, including one retraction made *after* a result was frozen (§7).
- *Novelty audit*: before claiming a contribution we actively check whether it is already published or merely a renaming of existing work (§1a demarcates standard inference from the credential increment) — a commitment to seeking refutations of our own claims, not only evidence for them.

## 3. The methodology contribution — auditable causal inference: a minimum standard

*We state this as the paper's primary, longest-lived contribution. The experiments in §4–§6 are evidence that the standard is **necessary**; they are not the standard itself. A specific model's accuracy on the 2^k axis will date as models improve — but a methodological criterion, once adopted, compounds. We therefore write this section to be independently citable: a reader should be able to say "following the auditable-causal-inference standard of [this work], we report our adjustment set and an independent recompute path" without using any of our software.*

**The proposal, in one sentence.** *A causal-effect estimate intended for a consequential decision should not be accepted unless it at least (1) terminates exactly within bounded resources, (2) is independently recomputable, (3) carries an explicit justification, and (4) declares its regime and boundary.* Today, neither raw LLM outputs nor bare Bayesian-network library calls meet all four. The point of this paper is not that an engine can — it is that the four criteria are the right bar, and that current practice falls below it in two empirically demonstrable ways.

### 3.1 Criterion 1 — Terminability (provably exact within bounded resources)
The computation must be guaranteed to produce the exact answer — or to declare that it cannot — within a fixed budget, *independent of the question's combinatorial size*.
- **Failure case (empirical, §4.3):** frontier LLMs do not meet this. On exact P(Y=1|do(X=1)) with k confounders, accuracy collapses to ≈0 as 2^k grows (deepseek k=4, gpt-5.1 k=5, gemini k=6, claude k=7); the answer is delivered confidently but wrong, or not produced at all.
- **Why it matters:** a risk number that is "usually right but silently wrong when the problem is large" is precisely unusable for decisions whose stakes *rise* with problem size.

### 3.2 Criterion 2 — Independent recomputability (verifiable by a second implementation)
The result must be reproducible to tolerance by an independent code path or oracle, not merely self-consistent.
- **Failure case (architectural):** LLM outputs are not reproducible by construction — token sampling, context, and model version all move the number, and the reasoning chain is not a traceable derivation. Across the 1,207 SCM truths in this paper, our engine's values are recomputed by pgmpy (an independent library) to <10⁻⁶.
- **Why it matters:** "trust the output" is not verification. Recomputability turns a claim into a checkable fact — and is exactly what model-risk validation (e.g. SR 11-7) demands of any model output.

### 3.3 Criterion 3 — Explicit justification (the credential, not just the number)
The result must carry the basis on which it was computed: the adjustment set, the back-door / front-door / IV path, the variables conditioned on — auditable by a third party who did not run the computation.
- **Gap in current tools:** standard libraries (pgmpy, DoWhy) return the *number*, not a structured, auditable justification of *why* that number. An auditor cannot interrogate "why this adjustment set and not another?" of a bare float.
- **Why it matters:** an auditor, regulator, or ethics board needs to interrogate the *reasoning*, not just check the arithmetic. This is the causal-inference analogue of proof-carrying code (Necula 1997).

### 3.4 Criterion 4 — Declared regime and boundary (certifies computation, not the world)
The result must state what it assumes and what it does **not** certify: that the causal structure is taken as given, and the CPT's calibration regime (e.g. "normal-times" vs "stressed").
- **Failure case (our own honest limit, §6.5/§7):** a credential certifies that the computation is exact *on the given structure* — it does not certify that the structure is correct, nor that the CPT reflects the regime that matters. We quantify this: a normal-times-calibrated credit model is fine on average (bias −0.05) but underestimates **stressed** default by 0.23–0.28 — the 2008 Gaussian-copula failure mode — *with a fully valid computation credential*.
- **Why it matters:** the most dangerous error is a confidently-exact answer to the wrong question. The boundary must travel with the claim, not hide in an appendix.

### 3.5 How the criteria relate to the failure modes
| Criterion | What enforces it here | What fails it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Terminability | exact enumeration engine | LLM combinatorial collapse (§4.3) |
| 2 Recomputability | pgmpy independent oracle (IPRG) | LLM non-reproducible sampling |
| 3 Justification | structured causal credential | bare numeric output |
| 4 Declared boundary | regime/structure field in credential | silent miscalibration (§6.5) |

### 3.6 The standard is engine-independent
This is essential to the contribution's reach. **None of the four criteria requires The One's engine.** Any causal-inference tool can satisfy them: pgmpy, DoWhy, bnlearn, CausalNex, or Tetrad can each perform exact inference (criterion 1), recompute one another (criterion 2), emit an adjustment-set justification (criterion 3), and carry a regime field (criterion 4). Our engine is the *first* system we are aware of that satisfies all four *by construction and as a single credentialed output*, but it is deliberately not the only system that could. The standard is the durable artifact; the engine is one conforming implementation.

### 3.7 Mapping to model-risk and regulatory validation
Criteria 2–4 align directly with existing model-risk and disclosure regimes: **SR 11-7** (US model-risk management — independent validation, reproducibility, documented limitations), **BCBS 239** (traceability of risk numbers), **IFRS 9 / CECL** (explainable expected-loss methodology), **ECB TRIM** (replicable internal models). The standard is not finance-specific, but finance is where its absence is already a *named, regulated* risk — which is why our case study (§6.5) is financial.

### 3.8 Honest scope of the proposal
This is a **minimum** standard, not a sufficient one. Meeting all four does not make a causal claim *true* — criterion 4 is precisely the admission that structure and calibration can still be wrong. The proposal is deliberately narrow and therefore defensible: *consequential causal-effect estimates should not be accepted unless they at least terminate exactly, recompute independently, justify their adjustment, and declare their regime.* The rest of this paper supplies the evidence that the first two criteria are routinely violated today, and that conforming to all four is achievable.

## 4. Layer 1 — Three vital numbers (known structure, given CPTs)

The engine is exact (1.000) on all three. Across the three, **1,207 SCM truths were independently recomputed by pgmpy**, max |pgmpy − engine| < 10⁻⁶ (AM-017).

### 4.1 The causal core (F-1)
A single confounded SCM with the frozen truth table P(Y=1|X=1)=0.82 vs P(Y=1|do(X=1))=0.70 (and four further assertions). All seven assertions are reproduced by pgmpy to a difference of **0.0** (`experiments/oracle_crosscheck/f1_oracle.py`). This is a sanity anchor: the engine does the right thing exactly where naive conditioning is biased by 0.12.

### 4.2 Complexity scaling
Three node-count tiers (5/8/12) × 150 instances × {A,B,C}. The flash-tier LLM degrades with causal complexity (large-tier accuracy 0.45–0.61) while the engine stays at 1.000; the separation has p = 3.5×10⁻¹⁸. A fixed-skeleton control that adds only *irrelevant* nodes does not degrade any system, isolating the cause as causal complexity, not node count. All 900 truths are pgmpy-verified and match the frozen formal-run values exactly (`scale_oracle.py`).

### 4.3 The combinatorial cliff (headline)
Sweeping k with the x-axis as 2^k, accuracy on the interventional query collapses for every LLM while the engine is flat at 1.000 (millisecond latency). We report the cliff on the *de-anchored* generator (the cleanest; see §6.4) as the main result: gpt-5.1 accuracy falls **monotonically** 0.64 (k=4) → 0.04 (k=5) → 0.00 (k=6), with mean absolute error rising to 0.24 at k=5; the engine is 1.000 throughout (IPRG pass, 4.98×10⁻⁷). Cross-family on the same generator, deepseek/gpt-5.1/gemini-2.5-pro all reach accuracy 0.00 by k=6 (Figure 1).

## 5. Layer 2 — Ecological validity

We relax the toy premises one at a time. Each step keeps the engine and the LLM on equal footing (same information) and asks whether the engine's edge survives.

### 5.1 CPTs estimated from finite data
Instead of exact CPTs, both engine and LLM receive the *same* table MLE-estimated from n ∈ {50,200,1000} samples (a fairness constraint: the LLM never sees raw data, which would conflate discovery with inference). The engine's error is exactly the irreducible *estimation floor* (the best any method can do with that data); it shrinks with n (0.076→0.018 at k=1) and, notably, does **not** explode with k, because per-cell estimation errors partially cancel under marginalization. The LLM adds reasoning error on top, so the engine's advantage **survives and emerges at k≥4**; at low k the two tie. This is the first end-to-end pass through the IPRG gate (360 instances, 4.99×10⁻⁷). `experiments/cpt_finite_sample/`

### 5.2 An imperfect adjustment set
If a confounder is *omitted* from the adjustment set, the engine still computes exactly — on the wrong structure. Omitting a weak confounder degrades gracefully (bias 0.009–0.043, still beating the LLM's high-k collapse); omitting a **strong** confounder yields a silent 0.30 bias delivered with a valid *computation* credential. This delimits the credential precisely: it certifies that the computation is exact on the given structure, **not** that the structure is correct (§7). `experiments/wrong_structure/`

## 6. Layer 3 — Supplementary findings

### 6.1 Prompt engineering cannot fix the terminability failure
We tested a minimal prompt (A) vs a full causal-inference scaffold (B) across three regimes, and the scaffold helps in none of them:
- **Struggling model** (deepseek-v4-flash, 12-node tier): the scaffold *hurts* — accuracy 0.447 (B) vs 0.613 (A), with higher latency and more protocol failures.
- **Comfortable model** (gpt-5.1, 12-node tier): no effect — A 0.967 vs B 0.933 (n=30), within noise; the model already handles the tier.
- **Collapsed model** (gpt-5.1, de-anchored k=5, deep in the cliff): the scaffold cannot *rescue* it — both A and B reach **0.00 accuracy** (MAE 0.145 vs 0.162, n=25); once 2^k exceeds the budget, no system prompt recovers it.

The unifying finding is stronger than "scaffolds are harmful": **prompt engineering — including the exact, correct back-door recipe spelled out in the prompt — does not solve the combinatorial cliff.** The failure is computational, not instructional; it requires moving the computation off the reasoning chain. `experiments/scaffold_crossbase/`

### 6.2 Ruling out the token-length confound (the decisive control)
The sharpest objection to the cliff — raised by an external reviewer, and warned of by *Frontier LLMs Still Struggle with Simple Reasoning Tasks* (arXiv:2507.07313) — is that as k grows the **prompt also grows**, so the collapse might be long-context degradation or intermediate-step arithmetic error rather than exact-marginalization terminability. We rule this out by **decoupling prompt length from marginalization load**: we pad a low-load (k=2, 2²=4) problem with *d* causally-inert distractor root variables, each adding a `P(Dᵢ)=…` line (tokens) but no edge to X or Y, so the do-query and its 2^k marginalization are unchanged while the prompt grows arbitrarily long.

- **Length sweep (gpt-5.1):** holding load fixed at 4, prompt length 66→111 words leaves accuracy pinned at **1.00**. Length alone causes no degradation.
- **Killer control (gpt-5.1):** k2 padded to **186 words** (load 4) scores **1.00**, while the *shorter* k5 prompt at **177 words** (load 32) collapses to **0.00**. The longer prompt is perfect; the shorter one fails.
- **Cross-family, clean (gemini-2.5-pro, 0 protocol failures):** k2 padded to **306 words** (load 4) scores **1.00**, while the shorter k6 prompt at **282 words** (load 64) collapses to **0.12**. Length is not merely controlled but *inverted* — the longest prompt is perfect — and the cliff tracks 2^k regardless.
- **Cross-family, strongest base (claude-opus-4-8, 0 protocol failures up to k6):** length sweep 66→**506 words** at load 4 stays at **1.00** throughout, and the killer control is the most extreme yet — k2 padded to **506 words** (load 4) scores **1.00** while the *shorter* k7 prompt at **483 words** (load 128) collapses to **0.12**. The longest prompt in the entire study is perfect; a shorter one with 32× the load fails.

Length is controlled — and, in the decisive pairs, *inverted* (the longest prompt is the most accurate) — across **three clean families** (gpt-5.1, gemini-2.5-pro, claude-opus-4-8), and the collapse follows 2^k in every case; the long-context / arithmetic-execution explanation is excluded, and the failure is exact-marginalization terminability. (Load-sensitivity also replicates on a fourth family, deepseek-v4-flash, but deepseek is *protocol-fragile* under long prompts — see §6.3 — so it is reported as an honest footnote rather than a clean length-control subject.) `experiments/cliff_token_control/` (NOTE-018).

### 6.3 Three crash signatures — architectural failure fingerprints
LLM failure on the cliff is not a single phenomenon: different model families collapse in **distinct ways** on the *same* 2^k axis, which is itself a finding — it means you cannot predict, across architectures, *where* a model breaks, *how* it breaks, or *whether it will tell you* it broke.
- **gpt-5.1 — confident wrong answers.** At k≥5 it returned 87/98 precise-to-4-decimals, unwarned, *wrong* probabilities (mean error 0.047, max 0.45), including mathematically impossible values P>1 (e.g. 1.0179, 1.28). It fails loudly-but-unflagged: a clean number, no warning, wrong.
- **gemini-2.5-pro — computational collapse without protocol failure.** Given a 6× output budget for its thinking tokens, it stays in-format at **0 protocol failures** throughout, yet accuracy degrades to 0.12 at k=6 — the reasoning terminates and emits a well-formed but wrong number.
- **deepseek-v4-flash — protocol / format collapse.** Under high load *or* long prompts the reasoning chain exhausts the budget and returns no parseable answer at all (e.g. 8/12 protocol failures on a long low-load prompt). This is a *format* collapse, distinct from gpt-5.1's *computation* collapse — the same combinatorial pressure manifests as a different failure mode.

All three are terminability failures, and together they make the credential's case sharper than any single one: **when the reasoning chain is not auditable, you do not know which of the three a given model will do — break loudly, break quietly-but-in-format, or refuse to answer — and only a credentialed, independently-recomputable inference lets you know, after the fact, that it failed at all.**

### 6.4 The anchoring artifact (a methodology contribution)
At high k, the true interventional probability concentrates near the mean of the visible CPT cells (a central-limit effect). A collapsed LLM that anchors on that visible mean lands near the truth by coincidence — inflating its apparent high-k performance and depressing its MAE. We verify this is an artifact, not capability, with two controls: a skewed-CPT generator (true value moves to 0.71, yet the artifact persists because it relocates with the mean) and a **de-anchored** generator (extreme confounder marginals spread the true value across [0,1] so no single anchor works). On the de-anchored generator the LLM's accuracy collapses monotonically (the spurious "k=6 recovery" vanishes) and MAE rises to 0.24. **Recommendation: benchmark high-k probabilistic reasoning by exact accuracy, not MAE.** `experiments/skewed_cpt_robustness/`, `experiments/deanchor_cliff/`

### 6.5 A financial application: credit-risk causal attribution
The combinatorial advantage applies where causal inference is genuinely *discrete*. Linear-Gaussian factor-return models have closed-form interventional effects and no cliff; **credit default** does not — default and firm distress are binary and multiple systemic stress factors create a real 2^k marginalization. We model k binary systemic factors (rate shock, liquidity freeze, credit-spread widening, …) as common causes of firm distress D and default Y, and ask P(Y=1|do(D=1)) — the causal effect of distress on default under a stress intervention, adjusting for systemic confounders. Because tail events are rare (P(factor)∈[0.03,0.20]), the true value is naturally de-anchored. gpt-5.1 collapses (accuracy 0.32 → 0.04 → 0.00 at k=4,5,6) with mean error ~0.10 on a default probability — a capital/pricing-material error delivered with 4-decimal confidence — while the engine is exact (IPRG 4.89×10⁻⁷). The value proposition here has two legs, and in finance the second may dominate: (i) the cliff (LLMs cannot reliably attribute multi-factor default risk), and (ii) the **credential** — a third-party-recomputable, auditable causal attribution that maps directly onto model-risk-management / regulatory validation (SR 11-7, ECB TRIM, IFRS 9 / CECL, BCBS 239). An LLM cannot meet auditability requirements *architecturally* — token sampling is non-reproducible and the reasoning chain is not traceable.

**But the engine is only as good as its CPT, and in finance that is the hard part.** We quantify the limit with a CPT-misspecification stress test that reproduces the 2008 Gaussian-copula failure mode: a CPT calibrated on *normal times* (weak tail amplification) versus the true *crisis* amplification. The engine computes do() exactly on either. The unconditional bias is mild (−0.05, because crisis configurations are rare and down-weighted), but the **stressed** measure — P(default | do(distress), ≥⌈k/2⌉ systemic factors firing), the quantity risk management acts on — is underestimated by **0.23–0.28** (true ≈0.66–0.72 vs miscalibrated ≈0.43). The credential certifies the computation is exact; it does **not** certify the CPT reflects crisis conditions. This is the financial form of the credential's limit (§5.2/§7): the honest financial product pairs the engine with a credential field stating the *calibration regime* (normal vs stressed) and the tail-dependence assumption — naming the real failure mode rather than papering over it, which is exactly what model-risk validation demands. The engine is *complementary* to standard methods, not a replacement: linear-Gaussian factor attribution has closed-form effects (no engine needed), and Double ML (Chernozhukov et al. 2018) handles high-dimensional unknown-structure confounding; the engine's niche is known-structure, discrete, tail-dependent inference. We additionally confirm the cliff is **not an artifact of toy structure** by reproducing it on a realistic **default-contagion graph** (the 2008 question proper: macro→industry→bellwether-distress D→downstream-debtor defaults Y_j, with systemic factors confounding D and each Y_j), computing P(Y_j=1|do(D=1)) — the contagion effect net of systemic co-movement — and sweeping the systemic-confounder count. The engine is exact and all truths recompute under pgmpy to <5×10⁻⁷ (IPRG pass, 4.91×10⁻⁷; an offline engine↔pgmpy gate of 24 do-effects matches to 5×10⁻¹⁶ before any LLM call). gpt-5.1 reproduces the cliff on this correlated-default structure: perfect at k=2,3 (load 4, 8) but collapsing to **0.40** at k=4 (load 16) and **0.20** on the portfolio-default-rate query, with a single anchoring-artifact recovery at k=5 (accuracy 0.60 but MAE only 0.009 — exactly the §6.4 effect, where rare tail factors concentrate the true value and the model anchors on it). The combinatorial cliff therefore appears on a realistic systemic-contagion graph, not only on the abstract back-door, with the engine exact throughout. Finally, to show the auditable-causal-inference standard (§3) is not a synthetic-only proposal, we instantiate the full pipeline on **real public data**: a 1,000-row credit dataset (German Credit) binarized into a 5-node SCM with CPTs estimated from real frequencies; the engine computes P(default|do(high-loan)) with a complete credential (adjustment set, back-door path, independent recompute, regime declaration), pgmpy confirms it to 5.6×10⁻¹⁷, and the LLM is accurate here too — as expected, since this is a small (2³) back-door where LLMs are still competent. The point is not a cliff but that *the standard is executable end-to-end on real data*, with the honest boundary stated in the credential: the structure is a domain assumption (not discovered), the adjustment set is necessarily incomplete (income, sector, macro confounders are unobserved), so the credential certifies the computation, not the effect's unbiasedness. `experiments/finance_credit_risk/`, `experiments/finance_cpt_misspec/`, `experiments/finance_cascade/`, `experiments/finance_real_data/`

### 6.6 Efficiency
The engine costs ~0 tokens and ~0.07s. LLMs spend 63–79% of their tokens on wrong answers; at high k, deepseek spent 917k tokens to get 0/100 correct. Token-normalized accuracy (AM-013) makes the cost asymmetry explicit. (Scope: structured input.)

### 6.7 The LLM cannot tell when it is wrong (metacognition)
Self-reported confidence is the natural objection to needing a credential — "won't the model flag low confidence when unsure?" It does not. Eliciting gpt-5.1's own confidence alongside its answer on the de-anchored cliff: accuracy ≈ 5%, mean confidence ≈ 90%, and — decisively — **confidence when wrong (92.5) is no lower than when right (88.0)**. Self-reported confidence is not merely uncalibrated but *anti-correlated* with correctness; it carries no usable information about which causal output to trust. This elevates the "confident wrong answer" (§6.3) from anecdote to a systematic metacognition failure, and motivates the credential's design: it does not *ask* the model how sure it is — it provides verifiable (third-party-recomputable) correctness. The credential is externalized, checkable metacognition. **The anti-calibration replicates and refines across families.** On gemini-2.5-pro confidence is essentially flat at ~100 (99.6 when right vs 99.4 when wrong) while accuracy falls 0.88→0.62→0.00 from k=4 to k=6 — at k=6 it is wrong on every instance yet reports 99% confidence. And on deepseek-v4-flash *given enough budget to actually compute* (16k tokens, exact through k=5), confidence is similarly high (~97) — but now it is *right*, so the same flat-high confidence happens to be well-calibrated. The invariant, then, is sharper than "anti-calibration": **self-reported confidence is a near-constant high value (~90–100) decoupled from whether the exact computation was actually completed** — it merely *happens* to be calibrated when the model is right and is dangerously anti-calibrated when it is wrong. Either way it carries no information about whether *this* answer finished the computation, which is exactly what an external, recomputable credential supplies. Importantly, the anti-calibration is specific to **hard-output** families that emit a number even when they cannot compute (gpt-5.1, gemini). deepseek-v4-flash does *not*: pushed past its reach (k=8 at 32k), it protocol-failed on all 12 instances — it declines to emit a number rather than emitting a confident wrong one (its §6.3 signature). So deepseek's metacognitive failure takes a different, arguably more honest form (refusal-to-output ≈ an implicit "I cannot"), and it never commits the *confident wrong answer* that the credential is designed to catch. (Honest limit: the eliciting prompt may itself lower accuracy; and because deepseek either computes exactly (k≤7) or protocol-fails (k≥8), its confidence-when-wrong is not measurable on this generator — but it is also moot, since it does not produce confident errors.) `experiments/metacognition/` (gpt-5.1: `run.py`; gemini: `run_gemini.py`; deepseek: `run_deepseek.py`)

## 7. Layer 4 — Boundaries and open challenges

Honesty travels with the claim.

- **The cliff has two distinct micro-origins, both sidestepped by the engine (a mechanism finding, twice self-corrected).** Sweeping the reasoning budget shows the cliff's origin is *model-dependent*. For **gpt-5.1** (a reasoning model), budget 2k→8k→32k tokens does **not** move the cliff (it stays at k=5), and at the cliff it spends only ~4k tokens *even when given 32k* — a **premature-termination** wall: it does not invest the available budget in serial marginalization (the same failure prompting cannot fix, §6.1). For **deepseek-v4-flash**, the cliff instead **recedes with budget and largely vanishes by 32k — exact all the way through k=7** (128 configurations), with protocol failures only *beginning* at k=7 (3/8) as even 32k approaches its limit — a **budget** wall: its collapse at our standard ~4k budget was largely truncation, not inability. Two micro-origins (premature termination vs budget exhaustion), one macro-phenomenon: incomplete exact marginalization. **Honest caveat for the headline:** our main cliff results use a fixed ~4k-token budget representative of normal use (and the abstract's claim is explicitly "within its reasoning budget"); a flash model given 32k recovers through k=7, so its 4k-budget collapse is largely truncation — whereas a reasoning model (gpt-5.1) does *not* recover even at 32k (it refuses to spend the budget). This *refines rather than refutes* the terminability framing — it relocates the phenomenon from "LLMs cannot compute this" to "at any deployable budget LLMs fail, and even at extreme budget they either refuse to invest it (gpt-5.1) or invest it to the point of token-blowup and emerging protocol failure with no verifiable guarantee (deepseek needs ≥32k and starts failing at k=7)" — and sharpens the engine's value: the engine is exact through k=7 at **~0 tokens and 0.07s**, versus deepseek's full-budget spend (and a reasoning model that will not spend at all). The cliff is governed by *how much exact serial computation the model actually performs* — which the engine performs exactly and almost for free, neither terminating early nor running out of budget. `experiments/cliff_mechanism/`.
- **The engine is itself O(2^k).** Exact enumeration also scales exponentially; the engine has its own far-out limit (k≈25–30). Its advantage window is "k large enough to break LLM reasoning, small enough to enumerate," which comfortably covers where LLMs fail (k=4–6) but is not unbounded. The claim is *exact-and-cheap vs approximate-and-expensive*, not *tractable vs intractable*.
- **Known-structure assumption.** All results assume the DAG is given. Whether the high-k-known-structure regime is common in real medical/financial data is a question for domain experts.
- **Low-complexity natural language.** When numbers are correctly stated in prose and k is small, the LLM marginalizes correctly itself and the engine offers no measurable advantage (k=1: 24/24 exact; k=2: MAE 10⁻⁴).
- **Qualitative natural language.** When the SCM is purely qualitative, the numeric comparison is ill-posed (a retracted result, REJ-002 / WEAK-03), and natural-language structure extraction is ~65% reliable.
- **A retracted result, kept public.** An earlier "engine wins on prose" finding was a two-canceling-bug artifact, caught and retracted *after* it had been frozen (REJ-002). It is recorded, not hidden — a test of the methodology.
- **The counterfactual-gradient bet (T4): a graded boundary within a form, a phase transition across forms.** Training a counterfactual model on one confounding regime and testing across regimes of increasing distance, transfer degrades *smoothly and monotonically* with shift severity (cf-MSE ratio to within-family: 1.0→1.5→3.7→9.9→14→30×) and **beats the pure-association baseline at every within-function-form shift**. Extending the shift to the *structural-equation form itself* (linear→nonlinear) reveals the true boundary — and, in the spirit of seeking our own refutations, **corrects the "beats baseline always" reading into a bounded one**. The outcome splits by whether the nonlinearity moves the do-ATE estimand off the subspace the linear observational features can encode: an interaction term X·U, whose expectation cancels, transfers gracefully (3.6× the within-form error, still beats baseline); a quadratic term X² shifts the estimand wholesale and **breaks below the pure-association baseline for the first time anywhere in this work** (cf-MSE 0.491 vs baseline 0.274, BCa-95 confirmed). So the refined, honest claim is: *partial* counterfactual logic transfers and beats pure association across confounding shifts and form changes that preserve the linear estimand, but a form change that relocates the estimand (quadratic) is a **cliff, not a slope**, and can fall below baseline. (Honest caveat: this is sensitive to how the counterfactual target is defined — the average-unit do-ATE we use is what makes the interaction cancel and the quadratic contaminate; we claim the qualitative boundary, not a universal cross-form degradation constant. `experiments/cf_gradient_crossform/`.)
- **Causal discovery (unknown DAG)** is the last unproven frontier, motivated directly by §5.2: because structural error is silent and unbounded, *how to obtain the structure reliably* is the decisive question for real-world deployment.

## 7a. The broader architecture this instantiates (context, not a core claim)

The causal-effect result is one instance of a single principle — *replace self-report with a machine-verifiable credential, and the value appears in the hard regime, not on average* — which we apply to two further faculties in minimal models: **memory** (credentialed retrieval on a de-confounded causal signature is immune to confounding, where flat-embedding retrieval transfers the wrong effect with error unbounded in confounding) and **metacognition** (abstaining under uncertainty caps confident safety mistakes that a loosely-tuned policy makes unboundedly). The memory result is the first *cross-pillar* evidence: the retriever's immunity is inherited from the computation pillar's credential, not re-earned. The memory pillar now runs on a **real persistent, versioned, sovereign store** (SQLite-backed, causal-signature-indexed, with hard-delete and export), the confounding-immunity reproduces under **real text embeddings** (text-embedding-3-small), and the store is wired into the end-to-end credentialed loop (`os_loop_sovereign`) — so this is no longer a pure simulation. At scale (a populated store of ~46 models including surface look-alike traps), signature retrieval stays 100% exact and regime-aware at ~1 ms/query while surface-embedding retrieval cannot disambiguate same-text/different-regime pairs (`pillar2_scale`). We present these as context for the architecture (the supplementary *Three pillars, one principle*), not as core claims — the causal-effect result is the research-grade one. The shared honest boundary (a credential certifies computation, not the structure/calibration) is identical across all three, which is itself evidence they are one system.

## 8. Conclusion

Our primary proposal is a standard, not a system: a consequential causal-effect estimate should not be accepted unless it terminates exactly, recomputes independently, justifies its adjustment, and declares its regime (§3). The experiments are the necessity case. We showed that the first two criteria are routinely violated today — frontier LLMs' interventional accuracy collapses in a sharp phase transition on the 2^k axis (a failure of *terminability*, not knowledge; universal across four families, with three distinct crash signatures, not a token-length artifact, and unfixed by prompting), and LLM outputs are architecturally non-recomputable. An explicit intervention engine is one conforming implementation: within a known-structure, given-CPT regime that comfortably contains where LLMs break, it converts causal correctness from a gamble into a machine-verifiable fact — exact at all combinatorial loads, milliseconds, ~zero tokens, third-party-recomputable — but the standard is deliberately engine-independent, and any causal tool can meet it. The boundary travels with the claim: a credential certifies computation, not structure or calibration, and the regime is bounded by enumerability and a known graph. The combinatorial cliff holds at any deployable budget and slides with budget/capability; removing it is either extremely token-expensive or impossible for models that terminate early — which is why moving the combinatorial part of causal computation off the reasoning chain, and certifying it against a fixed standard, is worth doing. A model's accuracy will date; the standard, once adopted, compounds.
