Accuracy vs cost — paying a fortune to be confidently wrong

Across 300 causal queries (k=1–6). The engine sits where you want to be: exact, at ~zero token cost.
Figure 3. Over 300 causal queries the engine is exact at essentially zero token cost; the LLMs are both less accurate and far more expensive. The asymmetry sharpens in the tail: at high complexity (k≥5) gpt-5.1 spent 565k tokens to get 11/100 correct and deepseek spent 917k tokens to get 0/100 — paying a large sum to be confidently wrong (63–79% of all LLM tokens went to wrong answers). This is the cost case for moving the computation onto a deterministic engine and certifying it: not just more correct, but cheaper and verifiable. (Scope: structured input; the engine itself is O(2ᵏ) and unbounded only within the enumerable regime — §6.) Reproducible: experiments/complexity_axis/.
