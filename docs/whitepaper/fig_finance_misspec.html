Auditable — but only as good as its CPT: the 2008 failure mode, made visible

Credit-default causal attribution. A model calibrated on normal times looks fine on average but silently underestimates stressed default. The engine computes both exactly; the credential certifies computation, not crisis-calibration.
true (crisis) default rate normal-times-calibrated estimate / faded = unconditional (≈ equal)
Figure 2. The engine computes P(default | do(distress=1)) exactly on whatever CPT it is given. A CPT calibrated on normal times (weak tail amplification) vs the true crisis amplification produces a near-identical unconditional answer (faded bars, bias ≈ −0.05 — crisis configs are rare and down-weighted) but a large gap on the stressed measure — P(default | do(distress), ≥⌈k/2⌉ systemic factors firing), the quantity risk management acts on — underestimating crisis default by 0.23–0.28 (true ≈0.66–0.72 vs ≈0.43). This is the 2008 Gaussian-copula failure mode: fine on average, catastrophic in the tail. The credential certifies the computation is exact; it does not certify the CPT reflects crisis conditions — so the honest product states the calibration regime in the credential (→ SR 11-7 / TRIM / IFRS 9 / BCBS 239).
